# Problem Statement

## Background

The pharmaceutical industry relies on pharmacovigilance to monitor the safety of medicines and on regulatory teams to prepare complete and compliant submissions. Pharmacovigilance teams work with large volumes of adverse-event data such as FAERS/AEMS, while regulatory teams review extensive pharmaceutical dossiers against the Common Technical Document (CTD) structure.

## The Problem

Identifying potential drug-event safety signals from large adverse-event datasets requires careful statistical analysis and review. At the same time, checking whether a pharmaceutical dossier contains the required CTD sections and identifying missing or unclear documentation is a time-consuming manual process.

These two activities are often handled separately, making it difficult to get a unified view of potential safety signals and regulatory submission readiness.

## Who is Affected

The problem primarily affects pharmacovigilance professionals, drug safety analysts, regulatory affairs teams, and pharmaceutical organizations responsible for monitoring medicine safety and preparing regulatory submissions.

These users need to analyze large amounts of safety data and documentation while keeping the results transparent, traceable, and suitable for expert review.

## Why It Matters

Potential safety signals require timely investigation because statistical associations can identify drug-event combinations that may need further expert review. Missing or unclear regulatory documentation can also increase review effort and create delays during submission preparation.

A unified and transparent workflow can reduce repetitive manual analysis, help prioritize potential safety signals, and provide a clearer view of documentation gaps before regulatory submission.

## Why Existing Solutions Fall Short
Safety signal analysis and regulatory submission readiness are commonly handled through separate tools and manual workflows. Analysts may process adverse-event data independently, while regulatory teams manually organize and verify large sets of documents against CTD requirements.

This separation makes it difficult to connect safety analysis with regulatory documentation review. Our solution addresses this gap by combining deterministic safety signal analysis, CTD requirement mapping, readiness assessment, gap identification, and AI-assisted explanations in a single platform.
